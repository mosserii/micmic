Privacy policy

Version {{version}}. Last updated {{updated}}. This policy covers the MicMic app for Mac and the MicMic app for iPhone, and this website. Using MicMic is also covered by our terms of use.

The short version

MicMic sends what it needs to answer you, and to speak the answer, and, unless you choose to share diagnostics, we keep none of what you say. Our server keeps a count of how many requests each device made and how much it had spoken, and, if you pay, a reference to your subscription. No recordings, no transcripts, no screenshots, no advertising, no tracking across other apps or websites, and no selling of data. We do not use your data to train AI models. When MicMic speaks in its natural cloud voice, the words of its reply go to ElevenLabs, which keeps them by default; choosing On-device voice in Settings keeps them on your device (see Spoken replies). On iPhone, our server also keeps what MicMic remembers for you, such as notes you ask it to keep, until you delete it or stop using MicMic for 30 days (see On iPhone). If you turn on Share diagnostics on iPhone, a record of your recent requests is sent to us so that we can fix problems, and kept for 30 days (see Diagnostics).

No account, just a device

MicMic does not ask you to create an account, and does not ask for your name, email address or phone number. The first time it runs, the app gets a random device id and a secret access token from our server. The token stays on your device (on iPhone, in the iPhone's Keychain) and proves to our server that a request comes from your copy of MicMic. Your plan and your usage belong to that device id.

On Mac: what reaches our servers

When you speak to MicMic, your voice is turned into text. In English and Hebrew this happens on your Mac: English by a speech model MicMic downloads to your Mac (macOS's own on-device recognizer is used until it is ready), Hebrew by macOS on your Mac. When macOS has no on-device recognizer for your language, which today is often the case for Arabic and Russian, macOS sends your voice to Apple's speech service to turn it into text, under Apple's privacy policy. MicMic itself never uploads or keeps a recording.

That text is sent to our server to work out what you asked for, together with what MicMic needs to understand it: the date and time, names from your contacts that it can match a name against (a list of names only, never more than 250 and usually far fewer), what is playing, what just happened in this conversation, a message it is preparing for you, the kinds of things you usually ask for, any saved preference that the request touches, and details you told MicMic about yourself, such as your home city, when a request needs them. Phone numbers and email addresses from your contacts are not sent for this; a number you say out loud is part of the text of your request.

  • Your screen. When you ask about your screen, the text on the screen, and for some questions a picture of the window in front, is sent too. When you ask MicMic to guide you through something step by step, the same is sent once for each step, and never while a password field is being typed in (then only the names of the buttons and fields go, without a picture).
  • Websites. When you ask MicMic to do something on a website for you, such as finding a table or filling in a booking, the text and the buttons and fields of each page are sent to work out the next step. MicMic never types into a password, card or identity document field, and never presses the button that pays.
  • Your messages. When you ask MicMic to read your messages, it reads them on your Mac, from Messages or WhatsApp, only with the permission you give macOS. The messages it reads to you are spoken as described under Spoken replies.
  • Your device. When MicMic first connects, it sends the kind of Mac it runs on (for example “MacBook Pro”, never the name you gave your Mac) and the app version. We keep them as a label for the device.

Our server passes each request on to the service that answers it and hands the answer back. It does not write the request or the answer to disk, and it does not put them in logs.

On iPhone: what reaches our servers

Before MicMic for iPhone sends anything to our servers, it shows you who receives what and asks for your permission. When you speak to MicMic on iPhone, your voice is turned into text. In English, once MicMic's own speech model is on your iPhone and switched on, that model turns your voice into text on the iPhone; iOS's own recognizer can listen at the same time, to show your words as you speak and as a backup. In other languages, in English until the model is on your iPhone, and if you switch it off in Settings, iOS turns your voice into text. Where iOS can do this on the iPhone it does; for some languages and devices iOS sends your voice to Apple's speech service, under Apple's privacy policy. The sound of your voice is kept in your iPhone's memory only while that request lasts, and is never saved or sent by MicMic. MicMic sends text only, never audio, and never keeps a recording.

Each request sends our server what you said or typed, and what MicMic needs to act on it:

  • The language you chose, your time zone, the local time, your region, the app version and the iPhone model and iOS version.
  • If you let MicMic use your contacts: the names in your address book (and nicknames), each with an id that only your iPhone can turn back into a person, and whether the contact has a phone number or an email. Phone numbers, email addresses and street addresses never leave your iPhone. Your iPhone fills in the number itself when you tap to send or call.
  • If you let MicMic use your location: the city and country you are in, never your exact position. It is used for things like the weather and places near you. The emergency number MicMic shows comes from your iPhone's region setting, never from your location.
  • If you ask about your calendar or reminders: the events or reminders you ask about.
  • What MicMic is playing, the message it is preparing for you, which permissions you have given it, and the battery level.
  • Something you share with MicMic from another app (text, a link, or a picture), only when you share it.

Our server works out the answer and tells your iPhone what to do, for example open a message ready for you to send. Your contacts' names, and the conversation itself, are held in the server's memory only while a conversation is going on, and are not written to disk. Requests and answers are not written to logs. The one exception is Share diagnostics, which is your choice (see Diagnostics).

So that MicMic can remember things for you on iPhone, our server keeps, under your random device id: notes you ask it to keep, preferences you set (such as which app to use for messages), the kinds of things you usually ask for, and the ids it needs to change or cancel a reminder, an event or a timer it made for you. You can delete all of it at any time from the app (see Deleting your data).

Calendar events, reminders, alarms and timers that MicMic makes are created by your iPhone in your own Calendar, Reminders and Clock, under Apple's terms. Messages, calls and emails are always handed to the iPhone's own Messages, Phone, Mail, or the app you chose, and nothing is sent or called until you tap.

On this website

This website runs no scripts, sets no cookies, and has no analytics or advertising. It is served by Vercel, which passes each request to our server on Railway. Like any website, they receive your internet address and the usual details your browser sends, such as the page asked for and the browser type, and may keep them in their logs for a limited time under their own policies. Our own server does not log internet addresses. When you buy MicMic Pro on Mac, the checkout pages are Stripe's, under Stripe's privacy policy.

Spoken replies

MicMic speaks its replies in a natural, computer-generated voice made by ElevenLabs, in every language it speaks: English, Hebrew, Arabic and Russian. The words of each spoken reply, which can include a message it is about to send, a message someone sent you, a name, or what it read on your screen, are sent through our server to ElevenLabs, which turns them into speech. A one-word direction for the tone of voice, such as "softly", can go with them. ElevenLabs keeps the text and the audio by default, under its own terms. Your own voice is not part of this: what you say is turned into text as described above, and only MicMic's reply is voiced. Choosing On-device voice in Settings keeps every spoken reply on your device: your Mac or iPhone speaks it itself, and nothing MicMic says is sent anywhere to be voiced. When there is no network, or the cloud voice does not answer in time, your device speaks the reply itself.

So that common lines such as “Alright.” are not made again every time, our server keeps the audio of a line once more than one request has asked for it. It is stored under a one-way fingerprint and encrypted with a key made from the words themselves, so it cannot be read back without already knowing the exact sentence, and it is not linked to any device. A reply that reads back your messages, your notes, your screen or a message about to be sent is never kept, and neither is a sentence written for you by Gemini. Your Mac keeps its own copy of such common lines too, so they can play again without a connection.

Diagnostics

When a request does not work, for example when MicMic did not hear you, it helps to see what happened. MicMic for iPhone keeps a record of your last 200 requests on your iPhone: how you started each one, whether the microphone was allowed and which microphone was used, how loud the sound was (a level, never the sound itself), which speech recognizer ran and the words each one heard, why listening ended, the text that was sent or why nothing was sent, what came back (the kind of answer, its card and the reply MicMic said), how long each step took, which voice spoke the reply, and any error. You can read it in MicMic's Settings, under Help, in Recent activity. It never contains audio.

This record leaves your iPhone only if Share diagnostics is on (Settings, under Help). MicMic then sends it to our server in small batches, under your random device id, and while it handles your requests our server also keeps its own record of each one: the words it worked from, what it decided and why, what it answered, and how long each step took. These records can include anything you said, including names. We use them only to find and fix problems in MicMic, never for advertising or to train AI models, and we keep them for 30 days, after which they are deleted automatically. Turning Share diagnostics off stops both records from being sent or kept from then on, and Delete my account and data deletes what was already sent.

Share diagnostics is off until you turn it on, in every version of MicMic. In test versions shared through Apple's TestFlight, MicMic asks you once whether you want to turn it on; nothing is sent unless you tap Share. You can turn it off at any time.

What stays only on your device

  • On Mac, in MicMic's folder in Library/Application Support/MicMic: your settings and preferences, your profile and what MicMic has learned you like, your notes, timers, a record of your recent requests (the last 4,000) that helps when something goes wrong, and up to 50 MB of common spoken lines. Your contacts book and your messages are read on your Mac and are not copied to our servers. Deleting that folder removes all of it.
  • On iPhone: your access token (in the Keychain), your record of agreeing to these documents, the record of your last 200 requests described under Diagnostics (sent to us only if you turn on Share diagnostics), the link between each contact id and the real contact, phone numbers, email addresses, your exact location, and your settings. Deleting the app removes them, apart from the access token, which iOS may keep in the Keychain until you use Delete my account and data.

What our server stores

  • For each device: a random device id, a one-way fingerprint of its access token (not the token itself), the device name and app version the app reports (on iPhone, the name is always just “iPhone”), when it was set up, and its plan.
  • On iPhone, a record that you agreed to our terms, this policy and the sharing with the services named here: which versions you agreed to, when, that you confirmed you are 18 or older, and the app language at the time.
  • How many requests each device made, and how many characters of spoken replies it had voiced, in total for the free plan and per day and per month for Pro.
  • On iPhone, what MicMic remembers for you, as described under On iPhone.
  • On iPhone, only while you have Share diagnostics on: the diagnostics described under Diagnostics.
  • The audio of common spoken lines, encrypted as described under Spoken replies, and for lines that may be kept, a one-way fingerprint with a count of how often they were asked for. None of this is linked to a device.
  • To stop abuse of free sign-ups: a one-way fingerprint of the internet address a device signed up from, with a count for that day.
  • If you subscribe on Mac: your Stripe customer and subscription ids, the subscription's status and renewal date, and the ids of the payment events Stripe sent us. Stripe holds your name, email and payment details.
  • If you subscribe on iPhone: what Apple tells us about the subscription, such as its id, the product, its status and when it renews or ends, and a random id we give Apple so that a purchase can be matched to your device. Apple holds your payment details; we never see them.
  • Server logs: the time, the kind of request, its result, how long it took, and the first characters of the device fingerprint or device id (for a payment, the device id and Stripe's event id). No request content and no internet address.
  • If you email us: your email and what you wrote, so that we can answer you.

Crash reports: MicMic uses no analytics or crash reporting service. If you choose in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give us crash reports and usage statistics, as Apple describes in its own privacy policy.

How long we keep it

  • What you say, what is on your screen, and MicMic's answers: not kept by us. Our server holds a request in memory only while it handles it. The exception is diagnostics you choose to share.
  • Diagnostics (only with Share diagnostics on): 30 days from when they reach us, then deleted automatically.
  • An iPhone conversation: held in our server's memory while it goes on, together with your contacts' names. A conversation with no new request for 15 minutes ends, and that memory is cleared.
  • What MicMic remembers for you on iPhone (notes, preferences, the kinds of things you ask for, ids of things it made): until you delete it in the app, and in any case erased automatically once that iPhone has not used MicMic for 30 days.
  • A device's record, its usage counts, its consent record and its subscription references: while the device uses MicMic, until you delete them in the app (iPhone) or ask us to delete them (Mac).
  • The sign-up address fingerprint: deleted once the day it was made is over (in UTC), at the latest when the next device signs up.
  • Fingerprints of spoken lines: deleted when nobody has asked for that line for 30 days. Stored audio of common lines: kept until the store reaches its size limit, then the least recently used lines are deleted first.
  • Ids of payment events from Stripe or Apple: kept so that the same event is never applied twice. They do not identify your device.
  • Server logs: kept by our hosting provider, Railway, for the period its service keeps logs, then deleted.
  • Emails to us: as long as needed to answer you and to keep a record of how we handled your request.
  • Records we must keep by law, such as payment and tax records: as long as the law requires.

Why we use it, and our legal bases

We use your data only to provide MicMic: to answer and carry out your requests, speak the answers, remember what you ask us to remember, count usage against your plan, take payment, keep the service secure and stop abuse, answer you when you contact us, and meet our legal duties. We do not use it for advertising, we do not sell or share it for advertising, we do not use it to train AI models, and we do not make decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects on you.

Where the law asks for a legal basis, such as the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), these are ours:

  • To provide the service you asked for (performance of our contract with you, GDPR Article 6(1)(b)): handling your requests, speaking replies, remembering what you asked MicMic to keep, your plan, usage limits and subscription.
  • Your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)): on iPhone, sending your requests to the AI services named on the consent screen and in this policy, sharing diagnostics when you turn that on, and the permissions you give your device, such as contacts or location. You can withdraw consent at any time (see Deleting your data and withdrawing consent); this does not affect what was done before. If what you ask MicMic includes sensitive information, such as something about your health, we process it only to carry out that request, on the basis of your explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)).
  • Our legitimate interests (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)): keeping MicMic secure and working, preventing abuse of the free plan, keeping minimal logs, and establishing or defending legal claims. You can object to this use at any time.
  • Our legal obligations (GDPR Article 6(1)(c)): keeping payment and tax records, and answering lawful requests from authorities.
  • Vital interests (GDPR Article 6(1)(d)): when you tell MicMic about an emergency, it may use your emergency contact to help you.

You do not have a legal duty to give us any of this data. Giving it is your choice, but without it MicMic cannot answer you or carry out what you ask.

  • Google (Gemini API) writes sentences when one is needed, such as an answer to a question or the wording of a message, and answers questions about your screen or a picture you shared. It receives the text it needs to write that sentence, and the screen text, window picture or shared picture when you ask about one.
  • ElevenLabs turns MicMic's spoken replies into speech, unless you choose On-device voice. It receives the words of each spoken reply and a tone of voice.
  • Stripe takes payment for MicMic Pro on Mac, and may sell it to you as the seller of record. It receives what you enter at checkout, such as your name, email, card and billing address, and your MicMic device id so that the payment reaches your device. Your card details go to Stripe, never to us.
  • Apple turns your voice into text when your device cannot do it on the device. This goes from macOS or iOS to Apple directly, not through us. On iPhone, Apple also takes payment for MicMic Pro, and tells us the status of your subscription.
  • YouTube and Apple's iTunes Search find the songs and videos you ask for. They receive the words being searched for, such as a song title, and the internet address of the computer asking, which on Mac is your Mac and on iPhone is our server.

Some requests are also looked up in public information services: the weather (wttr.in and Open-Meteo), exchange rates (Frankfurter), crypto prices (CoinGecko), stock prices (Yahoo Finance), news (Google News) and Wikipedia. They receive the words being looked up, such as a city, and the internet address of the computer asking. When MicMic opens a website or an app for you, such as YouTube, Apple Music, Spotify, WhatsApp, a search page or a shop, that service sees what you do there under its own terms. The Mac app also checks GitHub for updates, and downloads its optional speech and voice models and the components it needs from Hugging Face, the Python Package Index and the Node.js website, which see your Mac's internet address. MicMic for iPhone downloads its English speech model (about 464 MB) and an optional names add-on (about 103 MB) once, from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), if you choose to get the model. Hugging Face receives the request for those files and your iPhone's internet address, and nothing about you.

Apple and Stripe, when they take payment, and the services MicMic opens for you, decide themselves how they use your data, under their own privacy policies. We may share data if the law requires it, to protect people's safety, or to defend our legal rights, and with a successor if MicMic is transferred to another company or person as our terms describe, who must then protect it as this policy does.

Deleting your data and withdrawing consent

On iPhone: open MicMic's Settings, tap Account, then Delete my account and data. This deletes your device's records on our server, including usage counts, your consent record, any diagnostics you shared and everything MicMic remembers for you, and removes the access token from your iPhone. Deleting your data does not cancel a subscription bought through Apple: cancel it in your Apple Account settings first (see our terms).

Stopping the AI services on iPhone: in MicMic's Settings, under Privacy, turn off Use AI services. From then on MicMic sends nothing you say to our server or to the services above, and anything that needs them stays off until you turn it back on. Choosing On-device voice under Voice stops only the sharing with ElevenLabs. Turning off Share diagnostics under Help stops diagnostics from being sent and kept.

On Mac: contact us{{contact_inline}} and we will delete your device's records. If you subscribed, write from the email you gave Stripe; otherwise tell us the name of your Mac and roughly when you installed MicMic, so we find the right records. Deleting MicMic's folder in Library/Application Support/MicMic removes what it kept on your Mac. Choosing On-device voice in Settings stops the sharing with ElevenLabs.

Data that ElevenLabs, Google, TypeSafe, Apple or Stripe already hold is kept under their own policies; we will pass on a deletion request where they allow it. Records we must keep by law are kept for as long as the law requires.

Security

Everything between MicMic and our servers is encrypted in transit. Access tokens are stored only as one-way fingerprints, provider keys never leave our server, the database is readable only by the server itself, and stored voice audio is encrypted. We log as little as we can and keep no request content. No system is perfectly secure, but we keep what we hold small so there is little to lose. If a security incident puts your data at risk, we will tell the authorities and you where the law requires it.

Using your own key
